Editing rules

Do, don't, sometimes

DOEvery time

  • Cut on every new noun: shots near 1 s, at least 50 cuts per minute.
  • Show the literal thing being said whenever footage exists.
  • Keep a caption on screen 100 % of the time, centred at 50 % height.
  • Lock the two-tone watermark 56 px under the caption for the whole video.
  • Alternate the speaker's framing: 100 % → 112 % → 100 % → 120 %.
  • Open mid-claim with the caption already visible; end on the last word.

DON'TBreaks the look

  • Dissolves, whips, zoom transitions, glitches, light leaks.
  • Lower thirds, title cards, end screens, subscribe animations.
  • Moving the caption to the bottom third, or per shot.
  • Karaoke colour highlighting inside a chunk.
  • More than 2 s of B-roll unrelated to the spoken line.
  • Footage, music or podcast audio you have no rights to.

OPTIONALWhen the footage asks

  • Blur-fill letterbox for a source that can't be cropped (≤ 2 %).
  • Top/bottom split-stack for a two-speaker exchange (≈ 1 %).
  • Generated stills for hypothetical or comic beats, labelled where required.
  • 2–4 black frames as a gag accent.
  • Slow 100 → 106 % push-in on stills.

What couldn't be seen

Blocked during analysis

  • Video and audio streams: YouTube's bot check from a cloud IP. Not circumvented.
  • Transcripts (rate-limited) and storyboards for all but one Short.
  • Upload dates and durations for all but one Short (43 s).
  • Caption entry animation and in-shot camera moves, which still frames can't show.
  • Everything about audio: voice, music, SFX and mix are recommendations only.

Closing the gaps

Download 6–10 Shorts on your own machine and run the repo's forensic tools. They fill every field marked R or "unverified" in the JSON profile:

bash tools/forensics/run_all.sh analysis_input/
